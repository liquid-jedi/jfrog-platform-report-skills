Interpretation guide

Understand every report tile and decision signal

The report separates period activity, current posture, calculated metrics, external benchmarks and unavailable evidence. This guide explains what each section answers and how it should be used.

Observed

Collected directly for the stated period or snapshot.

Derived

Calculated in code from observed inputs.

Benchmark

External research, not a customer result.

Unavailable

A required source or boundary was not available.

How to read a tile

A tile is a compact evidence contract, not just a large number.

Value and unit

The normalized result and its measurement unit.

Classification

Whether the value is observed, derived, projected, benchmark or unavailable.

Risk color

Red indicates adverse evidence. Green indicates a favorable control result. Contextual change stays neutral.

What this means

A plain-language definition of the metric.

Why it matters

The leadership decision or exposure connected to the metric.

How measured

The source and deterministic formula, available by expansion.

Executive overview

A one-page synthesis of the week.

Executive signals

The few values that describe the reporting period.

Top risks

Adverse evidence requiring leadership attention.

Demonstrated outcomes

Measured control results, kept separate from projections.

Recommended actions

Actions with an accountable role and target horizon.

Trends

Comparable weekly archives show direction across the calendar month. Polarity is metric-specific. Fewer critical findings is favorable, while higher download traffic is contextual.

A trend is published only when two validated reports contain the same comparable metric. Current inventory is not backdated.

Curation

Requests evaluated

Dependency demand that passed through Curation.

Blocked requests and rate

Risky demand stopped before consumption, shown with scale.

Curation unique users

Requester identities using governed repositories. Identities are not treated as licensed developers.

Blocked versions and reasons

Distinct package risk and the policy conditions responsible.

Artifactory

Repository inventory

Local, remote, virtual, package-type and project ownership distribution.

Artifact storage

Physical storage at collection time and the repositories driving it.

Period downloads and uploads

Traffic calculated from two matching counter boundaries or complete logs.

Cache hit rate

Eligible binary-provider requests served from cache.

One cumulative counter is a baseline, not weekly activity. Missing boundaries remain unavailable.

Xray

New findings

Risk that entered during the period, separate from open backlog.

Severity and unique CVEs

Occurrence volume and distinct vulnerability diversity.

Indexed repositories

Coverage that qualifies confidence in the risk conclusion.

Top CVEs and repositories

Concentration that can be assigned to an owner.

AppTrust

Application and release-governance evidence is kept separate from Xray occurrence counts.

Application context

Projects, criticality, maturity and accountable owners.

Ownership gaps

Critical applications without an assigned owner.

Release flow

Stages, trusted releases, completed, failed and rejected promotions.

Evidence predicates

Verified and unverified release evidence.

Application inventory is fully paginated. A truncated nested connection is unavailable, never published as a partial count.

AI security

MCP servers, skills, plugins and models are shown as separate asset classes.

Behavioral status

Malicious, suspicious, vulnerable, not malicious, scanning, not scanned or N/A.

Dependency posture

Vulnerable dependencies remain distinct from behavioral scan status.

Project attribution

Linked, not linked or unknown ownership.

Governance and Shadow AI

Managed and unmanaged model adoption without inventing shadow skill usage.

N/A does not mean safe. It means the source did not expose a completed security result.

Access, tokens and OIDC

Rotation windows

Credentials expiring within 7 and 30 days.

Non-expiring and unused

Credentials with elevated lifecycle risk.

Safe rotation queue

Owner and expiry context without token IDs, scopes or secrets.

OIDC trust mappings

GitHub, GitLab and other workload identities trusted for repository access.

JAS and Runtime

Applicable vulnerabilities

Findings that remain actionable in context, including fix availability.

Advanced scanners

Secrets, IaC and SAST signals beyond dependency CVEs.

Running images

Production workload population and applicable exposure.

Integrity signals

Malicious packages or runtime integrity violations requiring escalation.

External Forrester benchmark

The optional final section shows the Forrester TEI composite only as external research. It is not derived from the customer's report, does not represent realized savings and can be excluded during section selection.

The skill never converts an external benchmark into customer ROI.

Recommendations and evidence notes

Recommendations connect report evidence to a priority, accountable role and 7-day or 30-day horizon. The final evidence section records source status, unavailable capabilities and known gaps so readers can validate the conclusion.
